Git

j shows a repository's state in a status buffer, as magit and Neogit do: what has changed, what is staged, and the recent commits, with each file's diff inline. j runs the git on your path for it, version 2.16 or later. To see the commands it runs, use vcsCommandLog ($ in the status buffer). The VCS menu, after the Mode menu, opens the status, the log, the current file's diff with what is staged, and the commit popup, from any buffer in a repository, and the command log.

The status buffer

vcsStatus, mapped to Ctrl Alt G, and to <leader>gg in vim edit mode, opens the status of the current file's repository. In a project whose directory holds several repositories, it shows them all, each under a heading in its own color. 

Head:     icons  1a2b3c4 icons: draw at the window's device scale
Upstream: origin/icons  ↑2 ↓0
Parent:   origin/main  (reflog)

Untracked files (1)
  ?  notes.txt

Unstaged changes (2)
  M  src/Display.java
  @@ -60,6 +60,12 @@ class Display
  ...
Staged changes (1)
  A  doc/git.html

Stashes (1)
  stash@{0}: On icons: half-done scaling

Recent commits (10)
  1a2b3c4 (HEAD -> main, origin/main) icons: draw at the window's device scale

The header has the branch, its upstream and how far ahead and behind it is (or gone), where it pushes when that is not its upstream, and its parent: the branch it is to be merged into, with where j found it in parentheses. Git keeps no parent, so j reads the one GitHub's CLI, git-town, git-machete or VS Code recorded, else the upstream when the branch pushes elsewhere, else the branch the reflog says it was made from, else origin/HEAD, main or master. Enter on the Parent line, or gitSetParent, picks another, which is recorded as GitHub's CLI records it, so gh pr create targets it too: origin's branch by its bare name, read back as the local branch of that name when there is one, else origin's; another remote's whole, as upstream/main. gitSetParent - removes the record.

The sections are those that have something in them: unmerged files during a merge, untracked files, unstaged changes, staged changes, the stashes (see Stashing), then the commits between the branch and its upstream, push target and parent each way (unpulled, unpushed, unmerged from the parent, and not in it), closed to begin with, as of the last fetch; and the recent commits, when no commit is on the branch alone. A section of commits between two branches shows the newest ten, then how many more, whose Enter opens the log of them all. A line under the header says when a merge, a rebase or a cherry-pick is in progress, and how many conflicts it has.

A dimmed row over the first line shows the main keys, in the notation of the edit mode you are in; vcsHints turns it off. ? opens a popup of the rest, in Show, Go, Changes and Commands, and F1 this page. The toolbar and the Mode menu have the main commands too: staging, committing, the diff beside and the log, and, side by side, the comparison's. Stage, Unstage and Discard are enabled when they would do something to what the caret is on, and Show Diff and Side by Side are pressed in while shown. With the diff open, Previous Change and Next Change go from change to change in it, unified or side by side, from the status too. In a side of the comparison, the status's buttons and items act in the status.

With the sidebar shown, its lower half has the status's sections: their files under their directories, a directory holding only one directory shown with it as one (src/org/armedbear/j), or their commits or stashes. The untracked, unstaged and staged files and the commits between branches are closed to begin with. What the caret is on in the status is selected there, or the directory or section around it while that is closed. A click goes to the section, file, commit or stash in the status, or a directory's first file, opening a closed section, and the diff beside follows; a double click or Enter visits a file or shows a commit's or stash's details.

Showing and hiding

A section, a file and a hunk each open and close: Tab on one, or on a line in it, toggles it, as does a click on the chevron in the gutter beside it. A file starts closed; opening it reads its diff. Shift Tab steps everything through four depths, and 1 to 4 go to one: 1 shows the sections, 2 their files, 3 the files' hunks, and 4 every line of the diffs. In vim edit mode za, zo and zc toggle, open and close, and zR and zM open and close everything. ]c and [c in vim edit mode, and Ctrl Alt N and Ctrl Alt P, go to the next and previous hunk shown.

In a diff, the words that changed in a line are on a stronger shade than the rest of it, when a removed line and the added line that replaces it have enough in common.

Moving and visiting

n and p go to the next and previous thing that opens, or commit; N and P, or Alt N and Alt P, and ]] and [[ in vim edit mode, to the next and previous section. Enter, or a double click, visits the thing at the caret: a file, at the line of the hunk or diff line the caret is on, or a commit, shown with its diff. A staged line is visited where it is now, after the changes made since it was staged.

The diff beside it

D shows the diff of the file at the caret in a window at the right, the status keeping the left of the frame, and hides it again. It follows the caret when the caret rests: a file's whole diff, the unstaged or staged one as its section says, or, on a hunk, scrolled to that hunk. A file can stay closed in the status. Space and Shift Space (or Backspace) scroll it from either side. In it, Enter visits the file at the line, Tab opens or closes a hunk, n and p go from hunk to hunk, s, u and x stage, unstage or discard the hunk at the caret or the lines selected, or the whole file on its heading, the first line, as in the status (see Staging and discarding), and q closes it, back to the status. Until it is read again after one, the next waits. With the vim keys, the hunk commands of a file being edited do the same there: <leader>hs stages, <leader>hu unstages and <leader>hr discards.

= switches the diff between unified, at the right, and side by side under the status, as vcsDiff shows a file: what is staged against HEAD for a staged file; otherwise, the file's buffer if it is open, followed as it is edited, or else the file, against what is staged. The window you are in stays the status, or the diff in its new form, at the same line of the file. In the status, with the diff closed, = opens it as it was last shown. Side by side, it follows the caret as the unified diff does, from each file's first change; in its two sides, Enter visits the file at the line, and q closes it, back to the status.

With it open, the status and its diff are switched to and away from as one, as the log is: going to another buffer puts back the windows from before, and coming back to either lays them out again. vcsStatusDiff opens a new status buffer with it.

Refreshing

The buffer is read again when the repository's index, HEAD or branches change, when you save a file, and when j's window comes back to the front. Ctrl R reads it again now, for a change j could not see. Reading it again keeps the caret on the file or line it was on.

Compare

vcsDiff shows the file being edited side by side with what is staged of it, across the frame: the staged text at the left and the buffer's at the right, saved or not, each in the file's mode and encoding, and read-only. A file not in the index, new or in a conflict, is all added. A changed line is shaded blue on both sides, the words that differ in it a stronger blue, an added one green at the right and a removed one red at the left. Between the two, each change is a band from its lines at the left to its lines at the right, narrowing to a point on a side that has none, where a line across that side in the change's color marks where its lines would be. Scrolling either side scrolls the other through the changes, keeping the lines at the middle of the two together, so that the side with more lines in a change scrolls faster across it and the bands bend as it does. With compareAlign, a side with fewer lines in a change has blank rows there instead, and the two scroll row for row. Runs of unchanged lines are folded on both sides, but for the three next to a change, with how many lines are hidden at the end of the line over each, or under it for one at the top. These are the diff's only folds, not the file's mode's: a click on that count or the gutter's marker, Tab on that line, or vim's za, zo and zc there or in the fold open or close one on both sides, and zR and zM all of them. A jump into a closed fold, as gg, G or :N, stops at the line with its count. Either side scrolls the other, by keys, the wheel or the scroll bar, and the caret moving on one side moves the other's to its line there, or to the line before a change's lines the other has none of, when that is in view. A comparison of a file follows it: an edit, a reload from disk or staging shows a moment later. n and p go from change to change (]c and [c in vim edit mode, where n and p are search's), and q closes it, putting back the windows from before. Outside vim edit mode, s unlocks the two sides, so each scrolls on its own, or locks them again, for every comparison and the session, and w ignores whitespace or compares it again (see Ignoring whitespace). In vim edit mode they have no key: the toolbar's Scroll Lock and Ignore Whitespace buttons, pressed in while on, and the Mode menu's items do the same, and the toolbar's Previous Change and Next Change go from change to change. Showing the same file again shows its diff anew in place of the old one.

diff FILE compares the buffer with any file the same way, FILE at the left; with no file, with its patchMode backup, or else a file it asks for.

Ignoring whitespace

diffIgnoreWhitespace makes diffs ignore whitespace, or compare it again: the status's, inline and beside, the log's, and every comparison, side by side. What it ignores is one of git diff's: whitespace at line ends (--ignore-space-at-eol), changes in how much there is (-b), or all of it (-w); each also ignores a missing newline at the end. The toolbar's Ignore Whitespace button turns it on and off, pressed in while on, and the arrow beside it chooses what is ignored; the Mode menu's Ignore Whitespace has the same choices, and w in a comparison, outside vim edit mode, turns it on and off. It holds until j exits, starting from diffIgnoreWhitespace. While whitespace is ignored, a hunk's lines are git's with it ignored, which can't be applied: whole files are staged, unstaged and discarded, and a hunk or lines are not.

Staging and discarding

s stages the thing at the caret, u unstages it, and x discards it: a section heading's files, a file, or the hunk the caret is in. They do the same in the diff beside the status: to its file on its heading, or the hunk the caret is in. With a selection, they take what it covers, down to single lines of a hunk: select the lines to stage and press s (in vim edit mode, V or v and then s). A changed line is its removed line and the added one that replaces it: selecting either takes both, so the change is staged and not the new line beside the old. S stages every change to a tracked file, and U unstages everything.

Discarding asks first, naming what is lost. An unstaged change goes back to what is staged; a staged one comes out of the index and the working tree both, unless the file has unstaged changes in the same lines, when nothing is done and the status bar says so. A file no commit has, untracked, added or noted with git add -N, goes whole: to the desktop's trash, or deleted where there is none. A staged rename is unstaged (u) before it is discarded. s on a conflicted file marks it resolved, after asking. While whitespace is ignored, only whole files are (see Ignoring whitespace).

While one change is being made, and until the buffer shows it, another waits: the status bar says so.

git runs in the background. When a command fails, the status bar says why and the command log opens with its output.

Committing

c opens the commit popup. Its switches are -a to stage every change first, -e to allow an empty commit, -n to skip the hooks, -s to sign off, and -v to show the diff being committed under the message, as git commit -v does; git's commit.verbose setting turns -v on too. Ctrl S in the popup keeps the switches as the defaults. Then:

  • c commits what is staged, with a message you write;
  • a amends the last commit, starting from its message;
  • e extends it with what is staged, keeping its message;
  • w rewords it, leaving what is staged as it is.

The message opens in a window beside the status, under the files it commits, as # lines that are left out of it. In vim edit mode it starts in insert mode. A summary line past 50 characters, and any line past 72, show where they run over. With -v, the diff is under a scissors line, and nothing from that line down is in the message. Ctrl Enter commits; Escape (in vim edit mode, in normal mode) gives the message up, asking first. In vim edit mode ZZ and :wq commit too, :q gives the message up asking first, and ZQ and :q! without asking. Ctrl P steps back through the messages committed or given up before, and Ctrl N forward again to the one being written. A message can't be closed while it is being committed, and one whose hooks fail is kept for another try. The toolbar and the Mode menu have Commit and Cancel.

Committing in several repositories

In a status of several repositories, c commits to each repository with something staged (with -a, any tracked change), one commit each, all with the one message, as IntelliJ's commit does across roots. Which repositories a commit takes is said everywhere before it is made:

  • the popup's line: commit to 3 repositories: x, y, z, or commit to x;
  • the message window's title: git commit: 3 repositories: x, y, z, or git commit: x;
  • the message's first # line, in capitals and the keyword's color (the theme's reach): # COMMITS TO 3 REPOSITORIES, ONE EACH: x, y, z, or # COMMITS TO x ALONE: y, z have nothing staged;
  • the files, listed under each repository's name, and with -v each repository's diff under its name;
  • the hint over the message: Ctrl Enter commit to 3 repositories: x, y, z.

Ctrl Enter commits to them one after another, and the status bar says each commit made, as Committed to x (1a2b3c4), y (5d6e7f8). One that fails, as when its hook refuses, leaves the others committed; the message stays open for the ones left, its title and first line saying which they are, and Ctrl Enter commits to them. Amend, extend and reword change the last commit of the repository at the caret alone, and their lines say whose (amend x's last commit).

Fetching

f opens the fetch popup. Its switches are -p to prune the remote branches a remote no longer has, and -t to fetch every tag. Then:

  • p fetches from the push remote, the remote of where the branch pushes;
  • u from the upstream's remote;
  • a from every remote;
  • e from a remote picked from a list of them, or a URL or path typed, fetched into FETCH_HEAD alone;
  • o only a remote's branch, picked from a list of them.

With several repositories, the popup has -a, all repositories: then p, u and a fetch in each, each from its own push remote, upstream's remote or remotes, one without that remote left and said so; e and o stay with the repository at the caret, whose remotes they list. Without -a, the lines say which repository they fetch in. A line whose remote isn't set says so, dimmed, and does nothing. A fetch runs in the background: the status bar says it is fetching, then how many refs it moved, or why it failed, the command log opening with git's message. It never asks for a password: a remote that wants one fails at once, unless a credential helper or an ssh agent gives it. It gives up after two minutes. The commits sections' counts are as of the last fetch.

Branches

b opens the branch popup, after Neogit's. Each of its commands asks for the branch, or where a new one starts, from a list, the branch checked out left out where it can't be the answer. The ref at the caret comes first: in the status or the log's list, a branch on the commit at the caret other than the one checked out, else a remote's branch or a tag there, else that branch, else the commit itself; in the log's refs tree, the ref the right button or the menu key (Shift F10) is on. So after c makes a branch from main, b b on that commit offers main first. Each list's label says what it asks for (Check out:, Start from:, Rename:, Delete:, Parent:), and its empty field says what to give it, dimmed. Where a commit will do, as for b, c and n, a hash or any ref git knows can be typed instead; with -a, only one from the list, a branch or tag name that each repository that has it uses, as a hash is one repository's.

  • b checks out a branch, tag or the commit at the caret: a remote's branch as a new local branch tracking it, or the local branch of its name if there is one; a tag or a commit detached;
  • l checks out a local branch, or a remote's branch that has no local one of its name, as a new local one tracking it;
  • r checks out a branch checked out before, the latest first, as the reflog has them;
  • c creates a branch, asking first where it starts, under Start from:, the ref at the caret first, else the branch checked out, or a commit's hash typed; then its name, a remote's branch offering its own; and checks it out;
  • n creates one without checking it out;
  • m renames a local branch, asking for the new name;
  • D deletes a local branch, after asking; if it has commits no other branch, tag or remote's branch has, asking again, naming them;
  • p makes a branch the parent of the one checked out, as gitSetParent does.

With several repositories in the status or the log, the popup has -a, all repositories, as IntelliJ's "Execute branch operations on all roots". With it on, b, l, c, n, m and D act in each repository at once. Their lists have the names any repository has, each saying how many have it: main (3), feature (2 of 3). A checkout or delete acts in each that has the branch, not one that has it checked out; a rename, in each that has it, checked out or not, as git allows; a new branch is made in each, starting where it is asked to in those that have it and at HEAD in those that haven't. A delete asks once, naming the repositories, and again if any would lose commits, naming them by repository. The repositories are done one after another, the status bar counting them, then saying where it was done, where not and why, and where it failed; one that fails, as a checkout with changes in the way, leaves the others done. r and p act in the repository at the caret alone.

Each runs in the background, the status bar saying what git said or why it failed, the command log opening with git's message: a checkout with changes in the way changes nothing. A deleted branch's commit is in the status bar, so git branch <name> <hash> brings it back. After each, the open logs of the repository are read again.

Merging

m opens the merge popup, after Neogit's. Its switches are -f, a fast-forward only, failing otherwise, and -n, a merge commit even when a fast-forward would do; not both. m merges a branch, tag or commit, asked for from a list labeled Merge: as the branch popup's are, a hash typed too, into what is checked out, with git's message. A merge that conflicts leaves the conflicts in the Unmerged section; then the popup has m, to commit the merge once they are resolved and staged, and a, to give it up after asking, putting back what was there before it.

With several repositories, the popup has -a, all repositories: then m lists the names any repository has, each saying how many have it, and merges the one picked into what each repository that has it has checked out; a conflict in one stops that one alone, and the status bar says where it merged, where not, and where it failed. Committing or aborting a merge in progress is the repository at the caret's.

Stashing

The Stashes section lists the stashes, newest first, as stash@{0}: On main: message. Each opens, as a file does, to its files, untracked ones too where git shows them (2.32 and later), and they to their diffs; nothing in a stash is staged or discarded. Enter on a stash, or on a line in it, shows its message and files over a file's diff beside the status, as the log shows a commit, and so does the diff beside when the caret is on one. Its parents are labeled: the commit it was made on, the index, and the untracked files. x on a stash drops it, after asking.

z opens the stash popup (Z in vim edit mode, where z begins the folds). Its option is -m, the stash's message, for the next stash alone. Then:

  • z stashes the changes, staged and not, leaving the untracked files;
  • i stashes them, keeping what is staged in the index;
  • u stashes the untracked files too;
  • s stashes what is staged alone (git 2.35 and later);
  • a applies the stash at the caret, or the newest;
  • p pops it: applies it, and drops it if it applied cleanly;
  • k drops it, after asking.

With several repositories, the popup has -a, all repositories: then z, i, u and s stash in each repository with something to stash, with the one message, and the status bar says where it stashed and where there was nothing. If any fails, the message is kept, and stashing again stashes in those left, the others having nothing to stash. Applying, popping and dropping act on the stash at the caret, which is one repository's.

Applying takes the stash the status showed, by its hash. Popping and dropping leave alone a stash that no longer has the name the status showed it with, as after another stash is made, and the status bar says to refresh. An apply or a pop that conflicts leaves the stash, and the conflicts are in the Unmerged section. A dropped stash's hash is in the status bar and the command log, so git stash apply <hash> brings it back.

The log

vcsLog (l l in the status buffer) shows the repository's commits, newest first, every branch's and HEAD's, and takes the whole frame for it: q closes it and puts the windows back as they were, each with its buffer, caret and scroll. vcsLogQuick (l q in the status buffer, <leader>gl in vim edit mode) opens the list alone in a window under the current one; Enter on a commit there grows it into the full log. vcsLogFile shows the current file's commits, through its renames. The toolbar and the Mode menu have its commands too: from commit to commit, and from change to change in the diff, unified or side by side; the filters, and, side by side, the comparison's.

The full log is switched to and away from as one, as a tab of its own: going to any other buffer from it, from the buffer list, :b or the switcher, puts the windows back as they were before it, and it stays, with each pane where you left it; going to it again lays the frame out as it was. In the buffer list its files and diff are under it, a click on one going to that pane; going through the buffers passes it once. q closes it.

In a project of several repositories, the log shows them all together, as the status buffer does: their commits in one list, by when each was committed, each repository's in its own order. A strip at the left of each row is in its repository's color; a hover over it shows the repository's path, and a click shows that repository alone (click again for all of them). vcsLogRepositoryNames names the repository beside the strip. A commit's files and diff are from its own repository, and they begin with its repository's name as a badge in its color, then its branches and tags as the list's badges. Two repositories of the same directory name are named by their paths. A file's log is always of the file's repository.

A row is the commit's place in the graph, its subject, its branches and tags as badges in a smaller font (HEAD's branch first, in bold), then the author and how long ago, which line up whatever the badges' widths. The graph draws each line of history as a lane in its own color, a commit as a dot on it, hollow for a merge and ringed for HEAD, with curves where a branch forks or joins. A lane longer than 30 rows is drawn as a short stub at each end, with an arrow, as IntelliJ does, and takes no room between; a click on an arrow goes to the lane's other end, reading on to it if it isn't read yet. A branch's badge is in its lane's color; a tag's keeps its own. A hover over a lane names its branch: the branch at its newest commit, or else the one its merge's message names, or else its newest commit. While an author, message or change filter leaves commits out, or the log follows a file through renames, there is no graph, as a commit's parents may not be in the list. In a narrow window the tags go first, then the remote branches, then the author is cut to initials, and then the subject; a hover over a row that is cut shows it whole. More commits are read as the caret nears the end.

At the right of the list are the commit at the caret's details: its id, author, date and message and the files it changed, and under them the diff of a file, against the commit's first parent. They follow the caret a moment after it stops, and the focus stays in the list.

= shows the diff side by side, the file as the commit's first parent had it at the left and as the commit has it at the right, across the bottom of the frame under the list and the files; = again shows it unified, and the next log opens with it as you left it. The window you are in stays where it is, or the diff in its new form at the same line. The sides follow the file at the caret in the files as the unified diff does; in them, Enter visits the file at the line, Alt Down and Alt Up go to the next or previous file, Space and Shift Space scroll, and q closes the log.

The refs

The sidebar's lower half shows the log's refs, the same from its list, files and diff: HEAD when it is detached, the branches, the remotes by remote, and the tags, newest first, the newest 25 and then a node for the rest, and the stashes, newest first; with several repositories, each under its name. The branches are open, the remotes, tags and stashes closed. A name with a slash is in a folder (feature/x in feature). The default branch comes first, the one the remote's HEAD names (origin's first), or else main or master, then the branch checked out, each by its whole name; then the rest of the branches, and the remotes, by name ignoring case, or with -o the newest commit first (vcsLogRefsOrder). The branch checked out is bold, and a branch with an upstream has how far ahead and behind it is after its name (↑2 ↓1), or gone when its upstream was deleted. What is open and selected stays so as the refs are read again.

A click on a ref puts the log's caret on its commit, reading on to it, and the keys are the log's again; a commit the log doesn't list, as one only a tag reaches without -T, a stash without -z, or one its filters leave out, is said to be so, with no page of the log read for it; moving the caret while it reads on stops it. Enter or a double click filters the log to the ref, as the branch chip does (a stash's is gone to, as a click does), by its whole name (refs/heads/release) when a tag or another ref has the same one, and to its repository when there are several. The right button, or the menu key (Shift F10), opens the branch popup, the ref first in its lists. Typing narrows the tree to the refs with the text in their names, and the label shows it; Backspace takes a character back, and Escape shows them all again, as they were opened. The refs are read when the log opens, again with Ctrl R, and when the tree is shown 30 seconds or more after they were last read; the filters' branches are the same read.

Filters

A bar over the list shows the log's filters, each a chip with its key: -b a branch or tag to start from (every one when unset), -a an author, -p a path (a file's history follows its renames), -s since a date (2 weeks ago, 2026-01-31), -g text in the message, -c text a change adds or removes, and, for several repositories, -r those to show. With several, a path is from the directory above them all (lib/src/), and only its repository is shown; a branch shows the repositories that have it. A click on a chip, or its key in the log, types its value in a field over it, with the branches and tags, the authors, or the files and directories listed under it as they match; a branch has a box of its lane's color before it, and a repository one of its color. Enter takes the one highlighted (one that is the text first, then those that start with it), the first unless you move with Up and Down, or the text when none is: erase a value and Enter clears it, as does the × after a set chip. The repositories are a checklist: Enter or a click checks or unchecks the one highlighted, and the log shows those checked a moment after the last, the field open until Escape, or Enter with none highlighted, as none is until you type or move with Up and Down; none checked, or every one, is all of them. ? opens the log's popup, its filters first, where the same keys set them, and x clears every one, as it does in a directory. -l folds each run of plain commits away, as IntelliJ's "Collapse linear branches": a commit is plain with one parent, one child, no merge and no branch or tag, and a run is two or more of them one after another down a lane. A run folds under the commit above it, which then shows … 12 commits after its subject and badges, its lane dotted where the run was. What is left is the history's shape: merges, forks and refs. Each run is a fold, folded or not, with its marker in the gutter on the commit above: a click on it, the fold commands (vim's za, zo and zc, from the commit above or any of the run's) and Tab open or close the one at the caret; zM and zR, Shift Tab, or -l, fold or show them all. A search that finds a commit in a run folded opens it. vcsLogCollapseRuns starts each log collapsed.

-T shows the commits only tags reach too: off at first, as a repository converted from CVS has a commit of its own for each tag, apart from its branch, which would each take a lane; a tag on a branch's commit shows on it either way. -z shows the stashes too, off at first: each stash@{n} a commit off the one it was made on, its badge its name, above it, its details' files its tracked changes, and its parents labeled; the index's and the untracked files' commits aren't listed. As a stash's base may be left out by a filter, they are shown only with none but the repositories set. The log is read again as each is set.

In the status buffer, l opens the log's popup with the same filters, to set before the log opens, as Neogit's does: -a an author, then l for the log or q for the quick log. They are kept for the repository, or for the project's several, so its next log starts from them too, opened from any of its files, and another repository's doesn't; Ctrl S in the popup saves them as its defaults.

Change marks in the editor

In a file git tracks, the change marks beside the lines are git's, in place of j's own marks of what changed since the file was loaded: a green bar for a line added, a blue one for a line changed, and a red triangle where lines were deleted, against what is staged. A change that is staged keeps its mark, dimmed, until it is committed. They are worked out again as you type, a moment after you stop, and when the repository changes. vcsSigns turns them off, and a file git does not track keeps j's marks.

The commands of Change Marks work on git's changes: nextChange and previousChange go by them, revertLines puts one back as it is staged, and previewChange shows it against what is staged, or the staged change there when there is no other. On the change at the caret, or the lines of the selection, too:

  • vcsStageHunk (<leader>hs) stages it, as the buffer has it, saved or not;
  • vcsUnstageHunk (<leader>hu) takes a staged change back out of the index.

The preview's bar and the popup a click on a mark opens have these too.

The buffer list

The buffer lists in the sidebar, and the Ctrl E switcher, show a file's status after its name: M changed, M in green staged, A added, ? untracked, D deleted, R renamed, C copied and U conflicted. gitBufferListStatus shows a dot instead, or nothing. When the project has several repositories, a strip at the left of a row is its repository's color. A buffer that is part of another is listed under it: the log's files and diff, the status's diff, and mail's message under its mailbox. A click on the arrow before one closes or opens it.
